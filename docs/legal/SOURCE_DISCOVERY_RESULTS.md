# Source Discovery Results for Placeholder Laws

## Summary

I've processed searches for all 100 placeholder laws. The web search results consistently return general information about Belgian legal resources (Moniteur Belge, Justel, BelgiumLex) rather than specific law URLs for each topic.

## Findings

### General Legal Resources Found (All Score 8-10/10)

1. **Moniteur Belge (Belgian Official Gazette)** - 10/10
   - Official publication for all Belgian legislation
   - URL: https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be (base domain)
   - All laws are published here

2. **Justel Database** - 9/10
   - Consolidated versions of legislation
   - Managed by Federal Public Service Justice
   - Accessible through Moniteur Belge

3. **BelgiumLex Portal** - 8-9/10
   - Searchable databases of legislation
   - Includes national legislation, jurisprudence

4. **European e-Justice Portal** - 9/10
   - Overview of Belgian national legislation
   - Links to official sources

## Challenge

Web search is not returning **specific law URLs** (e.g., `https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be/cgi_loi/change_lg.pl?language=fr&la=F&cn=YYYYMMDDNN&table_name=loi`) for each topic. Instead, it's providing general information about Belgian legal databases.

## Current Registry Status

- **100/100 laws** have URLs set
- **All URLs** point to generic base: `https://www.ejustice.just.fgov.be`
- **0/100 laws** have specific law page URLs

## Recommendations

### Option 1: Accept General Resource Pages
- Keep generic ejustice.just.fgov.be URLs
- Trust score: 10/10 (official domain)
- Users can search for specific laws on the site

### Option 2: Manual Search for Specific Laws
- Manually search ejustice.just.fgov.be for each topic
- Find specific law numbers and dates
- Update registry with specific URLs
- Time-intensive but most accurate

### Option 3: Use Known Law References
- For topics with known laws (e.g., RIS = loi 26 mai 2002), use specific URLs
- For others, use general resource pages
- Hybrid approach
